<h2 className="font-cormorant text-2xl text-zinc-100 font-semibold pt-4 mb-3">A Different World</h2>

<p>Leaving Peddamadi for Vizianagaram was the first major turning point in my life. For nearly fifteen years, my world had been bounded by the same quiet village roads, the same school, and the same familiar faces. Engineering changed all of that overnight.</p>

<p>I enrolled at JNTU-GV, Vizianagaram, as an Information Technology student. To be completely honest, I had no real idea what Information Technology actually meant. <em>Programming. Algorithms. Software engineering. Artificial Intelligence.</em> They were abstract concepts I had never encountered before.</p>

<p>Like most first-year students, my worldview was modest: attend lectures, clear exams, and head back home. I assumed college was simply a four-year bridge to a degree. I couldn't have been more wrong.</p>

<p>Programming entered my life at eighteen, starting with C. At first, nothing clicked. Compiler errors felt terrifying, every missing semicolon was a mystery, and practical sessions left me bewildered. I wasn't the student who picked up syntax instantly; I was just trying to survive the learning curve. Yet those confusing first programs slowly became the foundation for everything I engineer today.</p>

<h2 className="font-cormorant text-2xl text-zinc-100 font-semibold pt-6 mb-3">One Friendship Changed Everything</h2>

<p>Towards the end of my first year, I met Pravallika. Looking back, that single friendship redirected my entire trajectory.</p>

<p>While I was still wrestling with basic syntax, she introduced me to web development and pushed me to build real projects rather than just memorize textbook theory. Together, we decided to tackle something ambitious: building an Amazon clone.</p>

<p>We followed a five-hour tutorial line by line—pausing every few seconds, debugging obscure errors, and Googling endlessly until we understood why each function worked. It took us thirteen intense days. What seemed like a modest tutorial back then gave me something infinitely more valuable than code: it gave me confidence.</p>

<p>After that first breakthrough, learning became infectious. We moved from web development to machine learning, and eventually to Artificial Intelligence. Soon, our evenings were spent dissecting Large Language Models, computer vision, modern web frameworks, and emerging research papers.</p>

<p>Every hackathon, every failed prototype, every internship rejection, and every late-night terminal session quietly pushed us further. Curiosity was no longer just a childhood trait—it was becoming a career.</p>

<h2 className="font-cormorant text-2xl text-zinc-100 font-semibold pt-6 mb-3">Mentorship & The People I'll Remember</h2>

<p>One door opened another. Through Pravallika, I was introduced to Professor G. Jaya Suma—a meeting that became another pivotal turning point. She challenged me to look beyond syllabus requirements: to ask deeper questions, to publish research, and to explore advanced technologies most undergraduates never touched.</p>

<p>Many of the research papers, internships, and AI platforms I've built exist because a professor chose to believe in a curious student. Sometimes, all it takes is one person giving you an opportunity to prove yourself.</p>

<p>When I first arrived on campus, I was a quiet introvert who found starting conversations difficult. Thankfully, college introduced me to people who transformed those four years. My senior Mohan Pradeep mentored me through tricky crossroads, while hostel life introduced me to Das and Saroj—friends who quickly became brothers.</p>

<p>Our favorite memories weren't scheduled in calendars. A random evening conversation would spontaneously turn into a midnight ride. A free weekend would turn into an unplanned journey to Araku, Puri, or the Vizag coast. No itinerary, no plan—just shared laughter, freedom, and the simple willingness to say, "Let's go."</p>
