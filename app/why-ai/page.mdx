# Why I Chose AI

<div className="text-zinc-400 text-sm sm:text-base leading-snug mb-10 font-serif">From building websites to building intelligent systems.</div>

<div className="max-w-[62ch] font-serif text-zinc-300 text-[15.5px] sm:text-[16px] leading-[1.7] space-y-6">

<p>There was a time when I thought I'd spend my entire career as a web developer.</p>

<p>When I first started learning software development, my world revolved around HTML, CSS, JavaScript, React, and Next.js. Every new project made me a little more confident, and every completed website felt like a small achievement. I enjoyed building interfaces, connecting APIs, and watching ideas come to life in the browser.</p>

<p>At that point, I believed web development was where my journey would stay.</p>

<h2 className="font-cormorant text-2xl text-zinc-100 font-semibold pt-4 mb-3">The Question That Changed Everything</h2>

<p>Around that time, AI coding tools like v0, Lovable, and similar platforms started appearing. For the first time, I watched complete websites and applications being generated in just a few minutes.</p>

<p>My first reaction wasn't excitement. It was uncertainty.</p>

<p className="text-zinc-100 font-medium text-lg italic border-l-2 border-zinc-700/80 pl-4 my-6">"If AI could already build websites this quickly, what would software development look like five years from now? Would writing frontend code alone still be enough?"</p>

<p>Instead of ignoring those questions, I decided to understand the technology behind them. That curiosity became the beginning of my AI journey.</p>

<h2 className="font-cormorant text-2xl text-zinc-100 font-semibold pt-6 mb-3">Starting From the Fundamentals</h2>

<p>The biggest challenge was that I had absolutely no idea where to begin. <em>Machine Learning. Neural Networks. Deep Learning. Large Language Models.</em> Everything felt like a completely different world.</p>

<p>Rather than jumping directly into AI tools, I decided to start from the fundamentals. I enrolled in Scaler's Machine Learning course covering supervised and unsupervised learning. The course itself lasted around fifteen hours—it took me more than a year to complete.</p>

<p>Not because it was difficult, but because I wasn't just watching lectures—I was trying to understand every concept before moving forward. Learning became something I fit around everything else happening in my life.</p>

<h2 className="font-cormorant text-2xl text-zinc-100 font-semibold pt-6 mb-3">Learning Never Followed a Straight Path</h2>

<p>While I was studying Machine Learning, I was also working on internships, building production websites, participating in hackathons, and improving my full-stack development skills.</p>

<p>During that period, I worked on projects ranging from university websites to production-ready applications, including platforms for DSNLU, portfolio websites, and several client projects.</p>

<p>Some days I was building React components. The next day I was reading about gradient descent. Then deploying APIs. Then debugging production applications. Then returning to machine learning again.</p>

<p className="text-zinc-100 font-medium">Looking back, none of it happened in order. And I think that's how engineering really works. You don't master one topic before starting the next. You learn by constantly moving between them.</p>

<h2 className="font-cormorant text-2xl text-zinc-100 font-semibold pt-6 mb-3">Discovering Large Language Models</h2>

<p>Another turning point came through Professor G. Jaya Suma. She encouraged me to start exploring Large Language Models. Honestly, I wasn't very interested at first. Machine Learning already felt like a huge field, and LLMs looked even more complicated.</p>

<p>But once I started experimenting with them, everything changed. I began learning about prompt engineering, embeddings, vector databases, Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), inference, fine-tuning, local models, and AI system design.</p>

<p>The deeper I went, the more fascinated I became. For the first time, I wasn't just building software—I was building software that could reason, retrieve information, and interact with users in completely new ways.</p>

<h2 className="font-cormorant text-2xl text-zinc-100 font-semibold pt-6 mb-3">Going Beyond AI</h2>

<p>As my interest in AI grew, so did my curiosity about everything surrounding it. With guidance from Joel, I explored technologies that supported modern AI systems—from Three.js and CesiumJS to browser automation, GPU optimization, navigation systems, local inference, and developer tooling.</p>

<p>I wasn't interested in learning a single framework. I wanted to understand the entire ecosystem: how models run, how agents communicate, how applications scale, and how intelligent software is actually built. That mindset pushed me far beyond tutorials and into building complete systems.</p>

<h2 className="font-cormorant text-2xl text-zinc-100 font-semibold pt-6 mb-3">Building My Own Tools</h2>

<p>Over time, something unexpected happened. Whenever I needed a tool, I stopped searching for one—I started building it.</p>

<p>A document workspace became Serea. A repetitive task became an automation. A YouTube downloader became a weekend project. Personal AI assistants replaced manual workflows. Cron jobs handled repetitive work. Small utilities slowly grew into products I used every day.</p>

<p>Instead of depending on dozens of separate applications, I found myself creating software designed specifically for the way I work. That completely changed my perspective. Today, I don't build projects simply to learn a technology—I build tools because I genuinely want to use them.</p>

<h2 className="font-cormorant text-2xl text-zinc-100 font-semibold pt-6 mb-3">Looking Back</h2>

<p>Looking back, I don't think I chose AI because it was the next trend. I chose it because it challenged the way I thought about software.</p>

<p>Web development taught me how to build applications. Artificial Intelligence taught me how to build systems that can learn, assist, and adapt.</p>

<p className="text-zinc-100 font-semibold text-lg">Today, my work combines both worlds. I still enjoy designing great software and full-stack development, but what excites me most is building intelligent products that solve real problems.</p>

<p className="text-zinc-100 font-medium text-xl pt-2 font-serif">And I have a feeling that this is only the beginning.</p>
